The Italo-Turkish War, 1911-1912

29 September 1911: Italy declares war on the Ottoman Empire to seize Tripolitania and Cyrenaica. The Italian fleet rules the Mediterranean, but the Libyan interior is held by Ottoman troops led by officers such as Enver Bey and Mustafa Kemal, and by the tribes of the Senussi order.

Players

  • Italy (green) moves first.
  • Ottomans (red) and Senussi (brown), allied as the Sublime Porte.

Victory

  • Italy: at the end of a round control 8 victory cities (its own 3 plus Tripoli, Benghazi, Derna, Tobruk and Rhodes, or any equivalent combination).
  • Sublime Porte: at the end of round 10 (or later) still hold at least one of Tripoli, Benghazi, Derna, Tobruk and Rhodes: Italy fails to impose peace. Alternatively, control 11 victory cities.

Special rules

  • Impassable neutrals: Egypt (British), Tunisia, Greece, Crete and others.
  • Suez Canal open: the Levantine Sea connects to the North Red Sea.
  • Dardanelles: passing between the Dardanelles and the Sea of Marmara requires control of Thrace.
  • Desert: irregulars, Senussi warriors and camel riders +1 defence. Jebel (mountains): infantry +1 defence too; no blitz.
  • Camps and zawiyas (Jebel Nafusa, Jebel Akhdar, Jaghbub, Kufra): produce as much as the territory is worth and are destroyed when captured.
  • Battleships take 2 hits and are repaired at the end of the turn.
